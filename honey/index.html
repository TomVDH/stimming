Honey coil

Why a falling thread coils

Honey speeds up as it falls, so the thread gets thinner on the way down. At the plate it has to stop, and a liquid this thick cannot splash. The thread buckles instead, like a rope fed onto the floor, and the fold swings round and round. Each ring lands on the last one, and the stack grows. From low down the thread is fat and slow and the coils are wide. From higher up the thread is thinner and faster, and the coils are small and quick.

Move the pour and the coil turns into stitches. Slowly, the rings smear into a row of loops. A little faster, they come out as figures of eight, then as waves. Faster than the thread falls, it pulls straight. Physicists call this the fluid-mechanical sewing machine. Honey holds each shape for a moment, because it flows thousands of times more slowly than water. Then the stack sinks and spreads into the pool.
