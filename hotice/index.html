Hot ice

Ice that comes out warm

Heat sodium acetate and it dissolves in its own water of crystallisation. Let the clear liquid cool in a clean dish and it stays liquid far below its melting point of 58 °C: supercooled, with nothing to start a crystal. One seed is enough. A touch, a speck of dust or a crystal on a wire, and needles grow from that point until they reach the glass.

The freeze gives back the heat that the melt took in, so the solid comes out warm, at about 54 °C. That is why it is called hot ice, and it is how a click-pad hand warmer works: the metal disc in the pouch starts the crystal. Boil the pouch, let it cool, and it waits again.
