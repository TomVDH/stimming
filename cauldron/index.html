Why stirred soup sinks in the middle

Stir a pot and every bit of soup tries to carry on in a straight line. The wall stops it and turns it, so the soup piles up against the iron and falls away at the centre. When the whole pot turns at one speed, the surface takes the shape of a parabola. In 1687 Isaac Newton used a spinning bucket of water to argue that turning is real: the water knows it spins, because its surface curves.

Heat comes in through the iron at the bottom. The soup there turns to steam, and the bubbles rise and burst at the top. In thin broth they are small and quick. Thick soup holds each bubble under a heavy skin until it is big enough to break through, so it rises slowly and bursts with a low plop.
