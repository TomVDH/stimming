Beach walker

Thirteen numbers that walk

Each leg is eleven rods on one crank. The rod lengths are the set the Dutch artist Theo Jansen found in the early 1990s, when he let a computer breed thousands of legs and kept the ones whose feet moved best. Turn the crank once and the foot draws a closed loop: a long flat line along the bottom, then a quick lift and a swing back.

The flat line is the trick. While a foot is on it, the foot moves level, so the body glides instead of bobbing. Mirrored legs share each crank pin, and the pins sit at even steps around the shaft, so some foot is always flat on the sand. Legs adds pairs and spreads their phases around the turn.
