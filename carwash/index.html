Car wash

Twenty metres of water, cloth and air

In a tunnel car wash the car stays in neutral, and a roller in the floor pushes one wheel along at about a third of a metre a second. Each arch does one job. Pre-soak loosens the road film, foam carries soap onto every panel, then soft cloth scrubs: hanging strips first, then drums of cloth that spin round the sides and roll over the roof.

Soap works because each molecule has a head that likes water and a tail that likes oil. The tails grip the grime and the water carries it off. Wax does the opposite to the glass: it makes it shed water, so the film breaks into round beads. Beads are what the dryer's sheet of air can chase up the windscreen and off the roof.
