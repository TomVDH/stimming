Wind tunnel

Whirls that take turns

Around 1900 Étienne-Jules Marey built a box with dozens of parallel threads of smoke and photographed them flowing past plates and wings. The threads draw what the air does. At a crawl they bend smoothly round an object and close up behind it.

Faster, the air behind the object rolls into whirls that peel off in turn, one from each side, and drift away in two staggered rows. In 1911 Theodore von Kármán showed why only the staggered street lasts. Its beat is steady: about a fifth of the wind speed divided by the width of the object. Wires sing in a breeze at that beat. Wind takes the tunnel from smooth, through shedding, to torn.
