Jacob's ladder

Hot air climbs, and the arc climbs with it

A Jacob's ladder is two bare wires in a V on a high-voltage transformer. At the foot of the V the wires are only millimetres apart. There the voltage tears the air open and a spark becomes an arc: a thread of air so hot that it glows and carries the current.

Hot air rises, and the arc rides up in it. Higher up the wires stand further apart, so the arc stretches, and a longer arc needs more voltage than the transformer can give. At the top it lets go with a crack, and a new arc strikes at the narrow foot. A breath moves the hot air, so the arc bends away from it.
