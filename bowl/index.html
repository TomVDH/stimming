Singing bowl

Why the water jumps at four points

A wand drawn round the rim grips and slips hundreds of times a second. Each slip pushes the bowl further into its lowest way of ringing: the rim bends into an oval, then into the same oval turned a quarter, and back. Four points on the rim move the most and four hardly move. The wand rides one of the still points, so the whole pattern turns as the wand goes round.

Water at the four busy points is shaken hardest. Past a threshold the surface breaks into fine standing ripples, and then it throws drops. No bowl is perfectly round, so each tone is really two tones a hair apart. They beat slowly against each other: that is the slow pulse you hear as a struck bowl fades.
