Cuttlefish skin

Colour pulled open by muscle

Each fleck in a cuttlefish's skin is a chromatophore: a small elastic bag of yellow, red or brown pigment, ringed by tiny muscles. Nerves run straight from the brain to those muscles. When they contract, they pull the bag open into a flat disc of colour; when they relax, it springs shut to a dot. A whole pattern can change in under a second.

Under the pigment, iridophores reflect blue and green by interference, and leucophores scatter white. Cuttlefish use this skin to hide and to speak. Cloud sends dark bands rolling across the body, often while the animal stalks a crab. Mottle breaks its outline. Males show Zebra stripes to rivals, and a sudden pale Flash with dark eye spots startles a predator. Cuttlefish appear to be colour-blind.
