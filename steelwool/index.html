Steel wool

A battery and a pad of iron

Steel wool is iron shaved into threads finer than a hair. Touch both terminals of a 9-volt battery to it and current runs through the few threads that bridge them. They are so thin that the current heats them until they glow, and then the iron burns. Each burning thread heats the threads it touches, so the glow creeps on through the pad.

Blowing brings more oxygen, so the threads burn hotter and the glow runs faster. What is left is black iron oxide: brittle, and heavier than the wool was, because it now holds oxygen too. Threads that got hot but did not burn grow a thin skin of oxide. It shows as straw, bronze, purple and blue, the colours a smith reads as steel heats.
