Why the goo comes out through the holes

Press a sealed bag of liquid in one place and the pressure rises everywhere inside it at once. Blaise Pascal described this in the 1600s. The goo cannot get smaller, so it must go somewhere. The net holds the skin flat under each strand, and the skin can stretch only in the holes.

A dome in a hole is hardest to push when it is a half sphere, as wide as the hole. With an even pull in the skin, a dome needs less pressure after that point, so a bubble that gets that far swells out fast. Large holes give way first. A fine net needs a firm squeeze.
