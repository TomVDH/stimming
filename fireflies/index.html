Firefly sync

Nobody keeps the beat

Along tidal rivers in Malaysia, whole mangrove trees of fireflies flash on and off together, all night. In the Great Smoky Mountains, one species fills the June woods with bursts of light that roll through the trees in waves. No firefly leads. Each one keeps its own clock and watches the others.

A firefly that sees a flash just before its own hurries a little; one that has just flashed waits a little. In 1975 Yoshiki Kuramoto showed that a crowd of clocks with slightly different speeds locks together only when this pull is stronger than their spread. Below that line they never agree. Pull moves the crowd across it.
