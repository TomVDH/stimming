Chladni plate

Sand on a singing plate

In 1787 Ernst Chladni drew a violin bow down the edge of a brass plate dusted with sand. The plate rang, and the sand jumped into sharp figures. He toured Europe with the trick, and Napoleon funded a prize for whoever could explain it. Sophie Germain won it in 1816.

Each figure is a mode, one way the plate can bend while it rings. Some lines on the plate stay still. Sand bounces off the parts that shake and comes to rest on those nodal lines. A higher note fits more waves across the plate, so its figure is finer.
