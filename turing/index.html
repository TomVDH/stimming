Turing skin

Two chemicals draw the coat

In 1952 Alan Turing asked how an embryo, the same all over at the start, knows where to put its spots. His answer was two chemicals that react and spread at different speeds. One makes more of itself. The other feeds it and runs out where it is used. Small ripples in the mix then grow on their own into a pattern with a set size.

This skin runs the Gray-Scott model of such a pair. A brush stroke adds the reacting chemical, and the pattern spreads from it until the skin is full. Stripes changes how fast the skin feeds the reaction. Spots split and spread, then join into mazes. At the end the feed spreads less along a grain in the skin, and the mazes line up as stripes.
