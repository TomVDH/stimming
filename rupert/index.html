Rupert's drop

Squeezed skin, stretched heart

Drip molten glass into cold water and it sets as a tadpole. The skin freezes first. The inside cools later and shrinks, and it pulls the skin in tight, so the skin is squeezed and the core is stretched. A crack cannot open in squeezed glass, which is why the head shrugs off a hammer.

The tail is the way in. Snip it and a crack reaches the stretched core, then runs to the head at about one and a half kilometres a second, forking as it goes. The whole drop turns to powder in well under a millisecond. Between crossed polarising filters the trapped stress glows as rainbow fringes. Prince Rupert of the Rhine brought the drops to England in 1660.
