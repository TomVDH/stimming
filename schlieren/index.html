Schlieren candle

A knife edge in the light

In 1864 the German physicist August Toepler sent lamplight through a lens, brought it to a focus again and let a knife edge cut away half of it. Warm air bends light by a tiny angle. Light bent onto the knife is lost; light bent away from it gets through. So the heat over a candle, invisible to the eye, shows as bright and dark streaks. Toepler called them Schlieren, the German word for streaks in glass.

Here a fluid simulation stands in for the air in front of the mirror. The flame feeds it heat; the warm column rises, wavers and breaks into eddies. Your hand pushes the air aside, and held still, it warms the air too. Colour swaps the knife for a filter that gives each direction of slope its own hue. Shadow takes the knife away: that is the shimmer a candle throws on a sunlit wall.
