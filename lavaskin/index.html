Lava skin

A skin that keeps breaking

Basalt lava leaves the vent at about 1,150 °C. In open air its surface turns to glass within seconds: a thin, dark skin with a silver sheen. Under it the lava stays molten and keeps moving, so the skin creases into ropes and splits into plates. Geologists use the Hawaiian word pāhoehoe for this smooth, ropy lava.

The colour of a crack is its temperature. Hot rock glows dull red near 600 °C, orange near 900 °C and yellow-white above 1,100 °C. A fresh break shows the hottest lava. Then the skin grows back over it, the glow drops through orange and red, and the crack seals black.
