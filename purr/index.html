Purr

Why the fur keeps your stroke

Each hair of a pelt grows at a slant. A stroke lays a band of hairs in one direction, and they stay there until something moves them. Hairs that lie away from the light show their long sides and shine. Hairs that lie toward it, or stand up, show their tips and the dark gaps between them. That is why a hand leaves a light or a dark track on velvet.

A cat purrs at about 25 beats a second. Muscles in its larynx open and close the vocal folds, and the air buzzes on the in-breath and on the out-breath. Here the purr is the same kind of buzz: a pulse of breath noise about 25 times a second, louder as your strokes keep a steady rhythm.
