# Star Chart (`starchart`)

Named Solar loop, then Star catalog, Aster and Spectral Sightings, until 2026-11-24 (Tom renamed it Star Chart, Dutch Sterrenkaart). The folder and address moved from `solarloop` to `starchart` the same night (Tom: "change slug too"); `solarloop/index.html` redirects to the new address, and saved settings carry over to the new id.

Main shelf, number 75, series Vistas, stage alpha, version 1.5 (Tom, 2026-11-24: "we are promoting this toy to main as well to great fanfare"). Idea 238. Bean `stimming-e35i`. Plan: `docs/plans/starchart.md`. The menu's hover circle shows `shared/stills/starchart.webp`.

A star's edge at a vast scale, graded like film. The look takes its cue from two kinds of film: the sun as a furnace, all gold and white light, and the slow, hazy, monumental frame of large-scale science fiction.
Seventeen stars (Tom, 2026-11-23): one for each class on the main sequence, O to M, two brown dwarfs, and the stages of a star's life, from a protostar to a pulsar. Fomalhaut, a real ringed star, Beta Lyrae, an interacting binary, S5-HVS1, a star thrown out of the galaxy, and a Wolf-Rayet star, WR 124, join the life stages, and after the pulsar a Thorne–Żytkow object, the 1977 prediction; two black holes and a quasar follow them: Cygnus X-1, Sagittarius A* and 3C 273 (Tom, 2026-11-24). Ten more join for their looks and their extremes (Tom, 2026-11-24): Stephenson 2-18, WOH G64, a quasi-star, WR 140, VFTS 352, XX Trianguli, Epsilon Aurigae, Mothra, Earendel and Pōwehi. Mira, a pulsing red giant with a tail, R Leporis, a crimson carbon star, SGR 1806-20, a magnetar, the Crab Nebula, the Ring Nebula and Polaris, a Cepheid, join from Tom's picks (Tom, 2026-11-25). Every star is seen from the side, north up, turning about its upright pole (Tom, 2026-11-24). The surface lives on its own: granules boil (each lives about 7 s; on the red supergiants, a handful of giant cells), swirls writhe over about a minute, the supergranule network creeps, dark filaments. Above the limb, loops of plasma arc on field lines and drain back. Three giant prominences stand over everything: a hedgerow, a forest of fine threads, a twisted arch, and a tall curtain. No other bodies, apart from Mercury in transit on our sun (Tom, 2026-11-23).
Three views, stops on one zoom (Tom, 2026-11-23): the wheel, a pinch or - and = glide through them (long lens, wide view, full star, then out), and L jumps in Tom's order: long lens, full star, wide view. Every visit opens far out, at x32, and glides in, slow and methodical, to the full star once the intro has gone: 6.5 s on an even S-curve with no jolt at either end, outside the Zoom weight; the card plays 2 s after the view rests (Tom, 2026-11-24). The long lens fills the frame with the star, a fifth wider than at first so the limb reads as a curve (Tom, 2026-11-25); the old quarter cut, mirrored onto the west side (Tom, 2026-11-24): its limb arcs from the foot's middle to the right side's middle, and the bottom right corner is open space; the camera sways gently (Tom, 2026-11-24): a slow drift of about half a percent of the view, half as much on the wide view, none from the full star out; it eases with the zoom, stops when the toy sleeps, and is off under reduced motion. The wide view stands back from the same limb, upright. The full view shows the whole star. The star is active all round its limb. A raymarched volume of haze and dust, lit by the star with Fire's smoke shading, wraps every star; Mercury crosses our sun's disk.
The film touch is light: fine grain, a soft colour split, a faint lens dirt, highlights that roll off to white.
Tap a region and it flares: two ribbons flash, the core burns white with an anamorphic streak and lens ghosts, a rope of three braided plasma tubes lifts off as an arch, swells, parts and fades before its legs run straight (Tom, 2026-11-24: not wire, not smoke), a bubble swells. A new arcade snaps in, each loop one soft plasma tube with its own width, lean and shear, that cools and rains back. Each tube swells and pinches along its length; threads inside it wind opposite ways and cross, bright knots ride along it and its edge frays (Tom, 2026-11-25: not parallel lines). A tap's flare turns its rope and arcade square to the eye, so at the limb the arcade stands side-on as nested arches. A tap's flare grows with the view (Tom, 2026-11-25): from the wide view out to the full star its site grows to 2.2 times, its rope climbs as fast in proportion, and it burns brighter by the square root of that, so the arcade reads at the full star too. Tap a prominence and it erupts: it lifts and swells into space over about 15 s, then forms again.

## The black holes (bean `stimming-mhpz`)

Event horizon's black hole, ported into Solar loop (Tom, 2026-11-24): Cygnus X-1, a stellar hole of 21 suns, and Sagittarius A*, the hole at the centre of our galaxy, 4.3 million suns.

- A hole has no surface: the edge of its shadow stands where a star's limb would. Its radius is R, so the hole's units follow from it: R is 2.6 Schwarzschild radii.
- The disk pass traces one light ray per pixel backward, as Event horizon does: RK4 on the bend of light near a Schwarzschild hole. The rays run parallel, so the hole is seen from far away. Past 40 Schwarzschild radii the rest of the bend is the weak-field term.
- The disc lies in the hole's equator, from the ISCO (3) to 20 Schwarzschild radii (Tom, 2026-11-24: wider), tilted about 10 degrees toward the eye. Within the cloud layer the plane is 45 percent thinner, so the two never fight at one depth. Its far side arches over the shadow, a thinner arc runs under it, and each crossing adds light, so the photon ring forms. The gas turns at the Kepler rate in streaks that shear for one shared 12 s cycle and then a fresh copy fades in; the cycle starts later farther out, so no radius fades with all the others at once and no ripples build up over time. The outer gas is thinner, so it never reads as dark blots.
- Gravity and Doppler shift the light (g to the fourth power). Hot, approaching gas takes the cool ramp: cream white on Sagittarius A*'s ember gold, ice blue on Cygnus X-1's lilac steel.
- Stars lie on a far plane behind the hole and are read along the bent ray, so they smear into arcs near the shadow. They come in two scales that cross-fade with the zoom.
- The long lens frames the shadow's west edge at the equator, where the disc crosses it; the wide view stands back from the same edge. The full view stands three times farther back than a star's, so the whole disc fits, and it keeps its full exposure. Far out the flare grows round the hole; its glow stays off the shadow until the shadow is a few pixels wide.
- A cloud layer (Tom, 2026-11-24: the volumetrics "not quite there", after MisterPrada's singularity, ideas only: the repo has no licence): a thick, puffy volume round the disc, marched along the bent ray, so the clouds bend with the lensing. Its noise is baked once at load into a polar 3D texture (512 longitudes, 128 log radii, 32 heights, five octaves, and an emboss: how much denser the cloud is a little farther out). The layer flares from 0.5 to 1.5 Schwarzschild radii tall; its top rises and falls with the noise; thin strata stack over its height. Faces toward the hole catch more of the inner disc's light; cloud between a point and the hole shades it; tops catch the light and the interior sits darker. The gas is translucent: it glows through its depth and dims what lies behind only a little. Samples sit 0.15 apart on one grid along the whole ray, from a plane in front of the hole, with a still offset per pixel, so no seam, ring or ladder shows. The clouds swirl on the disc's cycle and take its Doppler shift. Sagittarius A*'s clouds are denser.
- No loops, prominences, haze, corona, heat shimmer or glare (Tom, 2026-11-24: heat haze is a star's). Light rays and lens dirt do act: the dirt catches the disc's light round the shadow. A tap does nothing yet; dropping a star in comes next.

## The S stars (bean `stimming-bw0w`)

Sagittarius A* carries the S stars, the cluster that weighed the hole (Tom, 2026-11-24). They show only while the star card or the article stands (Tom, 2026-11-25): they fade in over 0.8 s and out over 3 s once both close; reduced motion shows and hides them at once. Faded out, they do no work: no step and no draw. Twenty-two stars on their published orbits: S1, S2, S4, S8, S9, S12, S13, S14, S17, S18, S21, S29, S31, S38, S39, S55, S60, S62, S175, S4711, S4714 and S4716.

## Frame loop

- Lively while a flare, an eruption or an egg runs, a press is held, the zoom moves, a drag turns the star, a target's hover or press light eases or a star switch dips.
- Drifting at 30 fps: the loops' life cycle.
- Asleep 60 s after the last input: the drift eases to a stop over 2 s and the loop stops. A target turning under a still mouse is not input, so it never holds the toy awake. The sound does not stop with the loop: it keeps its own idle clock (see Sound).
- A star switch clears every flare and eruption at the dark point: the new star starts calm.
- Hidden tab: the loop and both clocks stop.
- Reduced motion: no drift; the loop sleeps at once. A tap still flares, softer (70 percent) and slower (80 percent); the shot noise holds still. The zoom and the star switch jump.
- Slow frames step down up to three rungs (`shared/detail.js`): a lower pixel ratio with smaller volumes and black hole pass, then fewer bloom levels, then the haze and light-ray marches at 0.56 of their steps (72 to 40, 40 to 22), the baked haze then takes a still dither (Tom, 2026-11-25). Only back-to-back lively frames count: the 30 fps drift and a Dev frame cap are slow by design.
- The rung is remembered on the device (`stimming.starchart.rung`), so the next visit starts there and the glide in runs smooth. With none stored, a second, timed first frame guesses it: over 60 ms starts at rung 1, over 150 ms at rung 2. 20 s of lively frames under 1/55 s lower the stored rung by one for the next visit, never live. A Quality change clears it. `?rung=N` pins it for QA, with no watch and no memory; `__solar.rung`, `__solar.frameTime` and `__solar.load` read the ladder and the load marks.
- Loading (Tom, 2026-11-25, after Railgun): the intro holds up to 15 s and its load line moves in six steps: programs, the haze bake, the hole bake, the quasar bakes, the first star's disk pass, the first frame. The first toy with load notes: over the line the intro reads Compiling shaders, Baking the haze, Baking the black holes, Baking the quasar, Compiling (the star's name), Drawing the first frame, each with its count. Each bake finishes on the GPU (a fence) before the next starts. The first star's disk pass links before the first frame, so the intro never lifts on black. The article text (`articles.js`) loads in idle time after the first frame; an article opened before then waits for it. The other stars' disk passes compile one at a time from the end of the glide in, only while the star is still; without parallel compile each one links on its pick instead.

## Sound

Plays by default (Tom, 2026-11-25); a mute is stored and holds, so a muted boot makes no audio context at all. Any key or a click wakes the sound. Space makes no sound, so this is a sonification: each part has a visible cause and pans with its region.
