Oscilloscope

Two channels, one beam

In X–Y mode an oscilloscope stops drawing time. The left channel pushes the beam across and the right channel pushes it up, so a stereo signal draws a shape. A circle is two sine waves a quarter turn apart.

Your stroke is walked at a steady pace, and one lap becomes one cycle of the tone. Pitch sets the laps per second. The phosphor glows hardest where the beam slows, so turning points burn bright. Spin mixes depth into the left channel; Morph blends two drawings, sample by sample.
